## A question worth measuring

A greenhouse roof admits sunlight unevenly. A beam can fall across one bed while a roof support shades the next. Before moving plants, compare the light that reaches both places over the same day.

Use two sensors at the same height. Keep their orientation unchanged and record both readings at each time. A single bright reading describes a moment;
it does not describe the whole day.

## A small set of observations

The table contains illustrative readings from two beds. Both receive their highest reading at noon, but the right bed receives more light in the morning. Record units alongside the numbers so that another reader can interpret them.

## Reading the pattern

The noon peak does not prove that the left bed provides better growing conditions. A useful comparison also depends on duration, cloud cover and the crop. Repeat the observations on several days before choosing a location.

## A better next measurement

Swap the sensors between the beds for a second run. If the difference follows a sensor, investigate its calibration. If the difference stays with the bed, inspect shade from the roof and nearby objects. Change one factor at a time.
